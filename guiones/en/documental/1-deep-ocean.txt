# A world without light
@energy 4
> Explain with confidence, pushing each sentence toward the key fact.

The ocean covers more than **seven** out of every ten parts of the planet.
But almost all of that ocean is deep, cold and dark.
At about two hundred meters, sunlight starts to fade.
Below a thousand meters, there is **no** sunlight left at all.
Down there, it is always night.
The water stays around four degrees, or even colder.
It's a cold that never changes with the seasons.
You couldn't see a rock, a fish or your own hand.
Plants can't grow, because there is no light to feed them.
[breathe]
And yet, life **refuses** to give up.
There are animals in every corner of that darkness!

# The pressure
@energy 4
> This is where the wonder goes: let it show in your voice.

Every ten meters you go down, the pressure rises by about one atmosphere.
In the Mariana Trench, the pressure is more than [pause] a **thousand** times greater than at the surface.
It's like balancing a car on the tip of your finger.
An ordinary submarine would be **crushed** in seconds.
For us, it would be impossible to visit without serious protection.
That's why exploring those depths takes robots and special vehicles.
And the animals? [up]
Many have no air pockets that could be squeezed.
Their bodies are soft and full of water, and they hardly feel the **weight**.
Some fish even carry molecules that protect their proteins from the pressure.
Evolution found a solution for every problem.

# Eating without the sun
@energy 4
> Curiosity: like someone sharing a good secret.

Without the sun there are no plants, so what do they eat? [up]
Much of the food falls from above, at a very slow pace.
These are bits of plankton, fish and algae that sink for weeks.
Scientists call it marine **snow**.
It's like a slow rain that never stops.
When a whale dies, its body sinks to the bottom and becomes a feast.
A single skeleton can feed hundreds of creatures for **decades**.
[pause]
In other places, life depends on the heat of the Earth.
Jets of water hotter than three hundred degrees shoot out of cracks in the seafloor.
Around them live **giant** worms, white crabs and huge clams.
All of that without a single ray of light!

# Lights in the darkness
@energy 4
> Quiet amazement, without exaggerating.

Many deep-sea animals make their own light, just like a firefly.
This is called **bioluminescence**.
Some use it to hunt, others to hide, and others to find a mate.
The lanternfish carries tiny lights along its body.
The anglerfish dangles a light in front of its mouth, like a fishing rod.
Some squid can even change the color of their light.
[breathe]
There are animals that release a **glowing** cloud to escape a predator.
In deep water, most animals can produce some kind of light.
It's a dark world, but full of **signals**.
Each flash is a message: I'm here, come closer or stay away.
For us it's a show; for them, it's a matter of life and death.
