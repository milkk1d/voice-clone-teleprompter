# Why the sea scares me
@energy 3
> Talk like you're with a friend, no rush.

I'll be honest with you: the deep sea scares me a **little**.
I'm not talking about the beach; I love the beach.
I mean that part where you can't see the bottom anymore and the water turns dark.
Look, the first time I swam far from the shore, I felt a hollow in my stomach.
Below me everything was blue and I couldn't see a thing.
And my mind started imagining huge things moving down there.
Has that ever happened to you? [up]
It turns out that fear has a name: it's called **thalassophobia{tha-la-so-FO-bi-a}**.
And it's a lot more common than you think.
So if it happens to you, you're not the only one.

# What hooked me
@energy 3
> Storytelling tone, with growing curiosity.

The funny thing is that instead of staying away, I started watching documentaries about the deep sea.
One night I stayed up until three in the morning watching videos of strange fish.
Fish with huge teeth, giant squid and jellyfish that looked like glass balloons.
Honestly, I couldn't stop watching.
It felt like finding a hidden treasure.
[pause]
There's a small fish that dangles a little light in front of its mouth to hunt.
Other animals glow on their own, in the middle of **total** darkness.
And I kept thinking: how can there be so much life where not a single ray of sun reaches? [up]
The next day at work, I couldn't stop telling everyone about it.
I think I became a bit unbearable about the topic.
Since that day, the sea still scares me, but it also makes me **curious**.
Both things at once.

# The fact that blew my mind
@energy 4
> Energy rises here: this is the most emotional moment.

Let me tell you the fact that impressed me the most.
We know the surface of the **Moon** better than the bottom of our own ocean.
Seriously!
There are maps of the Moon with more detail than the maps of our own seafloor.
[pause]
I mean, we have a huge world right next to us and we barely know it.
Every time they send a robot to explore down there, **new** species show up.
Animals nobody had ever seen.
To me, that's exciting and, at the same time, a little scary.
Because it also means there are things down there we can't even imagine.
Imagine everything we still have to discover!

# What I learned
@energy 3
> More reflective, slowing the pace a little.

Over time I understood something: fear often comes from what we don't know.
The more I learned about the ocean, the **less** it scared me.
Careful, I'm not saying I want to go down in a submarine now.
But I no longer see it as a black, empty abyss.
I see it as a place full of **life**, colors and secrets.
[breathe]
And I think that applies to a lot of things.
To a new job, a new city, new people.
When you understand something, the fear gets smaller.
It doesn't disappear, but it stops being in charge.
At least, that's how it works for me.
