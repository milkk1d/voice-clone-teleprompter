# My first bike
@energy 3
> Nostalgic and warm, like remembering with a friend.

Do you **remember** learning to ride a bike? [up]
I was seven years old and had a red bike with a little bell on the handlebar.
I lived on a quiet street in the neighborhood where I grew up.
My father ran behind me, holding the seat with one hand.
He kept saying: "Don't worry, I've got you."
And at some point, without telling me, he **let** go.
[pause]
I pedaled a few meters on my own, happy, not knowing nobody was holding me.
When I realized it, I fell right away.
I scraped my knee, but I got up laughing.
I had learned.
I still remember the sound of the wheels on the stones of the path.
It was one of the happiest days of my childhood.

# An invention without pedals
@energy 3
> Curiosity: tell the history like an anecdote.

The funny thing is that the **first** bicycles had no pedals.
In the early nineteenth century, someone in Germany invented a kind of wooden cart with two wheels.
You pushed yourself along with your feet on the ground.
People called it the running machine.
Look, it was almost like a giant scooter.
Imagine what people thought when they saw it go by.
Later the pedals arrived, but they were attached to the front wheel.
That's why some bicycles had an **enormous** front wheel.
[breathe]
Can you imagine climbing onto one of those? [up]
Falling from up there must have hurt a lot.
The bike as we know it, with a chain and equal wheels, came much later.
And since then, it has barely changed.

# Why I love it
@energy 4
> Energy rises here: this is what excites you most.

Honestly, to me the bicycle means **freedom**.
It's cheap, it doesn't pollute and it takes you around the city without depending on anyone.
And you get exercise without even noticing!
Plus, you don't spend a cent on gas.
Some days I get to work faster than by car.
While everyone waits in traffic, I ride right past them.
[pause]
Also, the city looks **different** from a bike.
You notice the trees, the smell of bakeries and the people in the squares.
Things you never see from a car.
It's a pleasure I still feel every time I ride.
You get to work with a clear head and in a good mood.
It's a whole other way to live the place you're in!

# The day I almost gave up
@energy 3
> More honest and reflective.

Careful, it's not all perfect.
One winter I had to ride through a **massive** storm.
I arrived shivering with cold, my clothes soaked and in a terrible mood.
That day I thought about selling the bike.
I even posted an ad, which I deleted that same night.
[pause]
In the end, what saved me was something very simple: buying a good raincoat.
I mean, the problem wasn't the bike; it was my **gear**.
Now I check the weather, carry lights and always wear a helmet.
And if it rains too much, I take the bus without guilt.
You don't have to turn every ride into a heroic feat.
The idea is to enjoy it, not to suffer.
