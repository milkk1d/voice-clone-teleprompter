# The trip that went backwards
@energy 3
> Funny storytelling tone, no rush.

Today I want to tell you about the worst trip of my life.
Well, the **worst** and also one of the funniest.
It was a long weekend and I planned everything weeks in advance.
Tickets, hotel and a list of places to visit.
I even booked a restaurant for the first night.
I checked the weather twice.
Everything was perfect, in theory.
[pause]
You can probably guess how it ended.
Because if something could go wrong, it went **even** worse.
Has that ever happened to you? [up]
The funny part is that today I tell it laughing.

# It all started at the airport
@energy 3
> Brisk pace, with humor.

The first problem showed up at the airport: my flight was **delayed** four hours.
Look, no big deal, I thought, I'll get a coffee and read a book.
But when we finally landed, my suitcase never showed up.
I waited and waited, watching the belt, and nothing.
Nobody could tell me where it was.
[breathe]
The woman at the desk said it would arrive "soon".
That "soon" turned into **three** days.
So there I was in another city with the clothes on my back, a charger and a toothbrush.
That's it!
And I was hungry and tired and just wanted a shower.
I had to buy a white souvenir T-shirt so I'd have something to wear.
It had the name of the city in giant letters.

# The rain and the hotel
@energy 4
> Energy rises here: this is the most chaotic moment.

And just when I thought it couldn't get worse, it started to rain.
Not a **normal** rain, but the kind that never stops.
I reached the hotel shivering with cold, my clothes soaked and my shoes full of water.
And guess what? [up]
My reservation wasn't in the system.
I had to wait an hour at the front desk, dripping on the floor.
[pause]
That's when I almost cried, honestly.
In the end they gave me a **tiny** room next to the elevator.
Until dawn, I heard the elevator going up and down.
It was a complete disaster!
And my souvenir T-shirt was the only dry thing I had.

# The unexpected turn
@energy 3
> More reflective and warm.

The funny thing is that the next day, everything **changed**.
Since I had no clothes and no plans, I went out walking with no direction.
I explored a market, tried local food and chatted with a very friendly vendor.
The locals treated me like they'd known me all my life.
He recommended a lookout that wasn't in any guidebook.
[breathe]
From there I watched the best sunset of my life.
It was a pleasure I never planned.
I mean, the trip I planned so much didn't work out at all.
And the one with **no** plan was unforgettable.
Since then I have a rule: I always leave a free day on my trips, to improvise.
And I carry a change of clothes in my backpack, of course.
Just in case.
