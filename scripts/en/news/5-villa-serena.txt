# The bakery's first day
@energy 4
> Local news, steady pace.

La Espiga bakery opened its doors this Monday at six in the morning.
Before dawn, there was already a line of **twenty** neighbors outside the door.
Its owner, young Rosa Fuentes, said she didn't sleep at all the night before.
"I've been dreaming of this day for three years," she said, her hands covered in flour.
However, the opening had a small **setback**.
At eight o'clock, the main oven stopped working.
Rosa and her assistant had to bake in shifts in an oven lent by the school.
The neighbors waited patiently, and no one left without their bread.
Among the first customers was the mayor, who ordered twelve rolls and a coffee.
By closing time, the bakery had sold more than **five hundred** loaves.

# The school trip
@energy 4
> Report without losing the serious tone.

A school trip to the science museum in the capital ended with a **change** of plans.
The bus left on time at seven, with forty students and three teachers on board.
Halfway there, a tire blew out in an area with no cell signal.
What did they do while they waited for help? [up]
The teachers set up an outdoor **class** next to a field of sunflowers.
The students counted petals, measured shadows and drew insects.
No one was hurt, and not a single student wanted to go back early.
The tow truck arrived three hours later, when it was too late for the museum.
One of the teachers described it as "the best science day of the year."
The school confirmed that the museum trip will be **rescheduled** for the spring.
[pause]

# A festival in the rain
@energy 4
> Stay serious, with a touch of joy.

The traditional harvest festival took place this Saturday under **heavy** rain.
The organizers had forecast sunshine for the whole week.
That morning, however, dark clouds covered the sky over Villa Serena.
At eleven, it started to rain, and it didn't stop until **nightfall**.
Instead of canceling the festival, residents moved the stalls to the town gym.
The food stalls sold more hot soup than at any other festival.
The town band played in soaked shoes, and the crowd danced among open umbrellas.
The prize for the biggest pumpkin went to a giant weighing **ninety** kilos, according to the official rules.
Nobody remembers a festival so wet and so joyful!
The organizers are already promising a giant tent for next year.
