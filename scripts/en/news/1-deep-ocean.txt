# Animals that glow
@energy 4
> Keep a steady pace.

Below a thousand meters, sunlight no longer reaches.
Even so, the deep sea is not in total darkness.
Many animals make their **own** light, a phenomenon called bioluminescence.
They use it to attract prey, confuse their enemies or find a mate.
That glow also helps them communicate with others of their species.
Lanternfish, the vampire squid and several jellyfish are among the best-known cases.
Researchers estimate that **most** deep-sea animals can produce light.
Their light is usually blue or green, the colors that travel best through cold water.
Some fish have **huge** eyes to catch the faintest glow.
Others have almost no eyes and find their way by touch and vibration.
