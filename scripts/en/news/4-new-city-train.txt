# Fares and schedule
@energy 4
> Clear, precise facts.

A ticket will cost the same as a bus ride, and you can pay with the same **card**.
Students and older adults will get half-price fares.
During the first month, Sunday rides will be **free**.
The train will run every day, from six in the morning until midnight.
At peak hours, a train will come every five minutes.
The rest of the day, the wait will not exceed ten minutes.
Bicycles can ride in the last car, except between seven and nine in the morning.
Every station has elevators, ramps and signs for people with **visual** impairments.
The operator expects the train to carry more than fifty thousand passengers a day.
[pause]

# What changes for residents

> A story with different voices, neutral tone.

For many families, the train changes their daily **routine**.
People who live in the northern neighborhoods will save almost an hour a day.
Shop owners near the stations expect more customers and more activity.
However, some residents of Long Street complain about the noise from months of construction.
The city government promised to plant three hundred trees along the line as **compensation**.
Less traffic downtown and cleaner air are also expected.
Experts estimate that thousands of cars will stay off the road each day.
Will that be enough to win over drivers? [up]
According to experts, success will depend on the service being punctual and **safe**.
