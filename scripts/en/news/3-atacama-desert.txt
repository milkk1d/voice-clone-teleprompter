Good evening, this is our special report from the Atacama Desert.
Located in northern Chile, it is one of the **driest** places on Earth.
Some weather stations in the desert have almost never recorded rain.
Its clear skies attract astronomers from all over the world.
Its soil, on the other hand, interests people who study the planet **Mars**.
And when it rains more than usual, something **surprising** happens: the desert blooms.
In this edition, we visit its observatories, its salt flats and its geysers.
All of it, just a few hours from the Pacific coast.
It is a land of contrasts, between the ocean and the Andes mountains.

# The clearest skies
@energy 4
> Report with confidence.

Altitude, dry air and a lack of clouds make the Atacama an ideal place to watch the sky.
That's why some of the world's most important **observatories** operate in the region.
Many of them sit more than two thousand meters above sea level.
To protect the dark sky, artificial light is limited near the observatories.
One of them stands at about **five** thousand meters.
There, technicians work with little oxygen and in temperatures below freezing.
Why go to so much effort? [up]
Because from there, galaxies, stars and planets can be studied in **unique** detail.
The night sky also draws thousands of tourists every year.
Many come just to see the Milky Way with the naked eye.
[pause]

# The flowering desert
@energy 4
> A story with a striking fact, without exaggerating.

Our next story is a phenomenon that doesn't happen every year.
When rains are heavier than usual, the desert is covered in **flowers**.
Seeds wait under the ground for years, sometimes for a decade.
After a few days of water, they spread across vast areas in purple, yellow and white.
The event is known as the **flowering desert**.
Some of these species exist only in this part of the world.
It draws visitors, photographers and scientists who study how these plants survive.
Authorities ask people to follow the rules: don't pick the flowers or leave the marked paths.
A single footstep can damage seeds that waited **years** to sprout.
[pause]

# Salt flats and geysers
@energy 4
> Keep a steady pace.

On the high plateau of the Atacama lie huge **salt flats**, vast white plains of salt.
Beneath them are some of the largest lithium reserves in the world.
This metal is used in the batteries of phones, computers and electric cars.
Mining it creates jobs, but also raises debate about **water** use in such a dry area.
The lagoons of the salt flats are also home to flamingos that feed on tiny organisms.
Further north, more than four thousand meters up, are the El Tatio geysers.
At dawn, dozens of columns of steam burst from the ground with great **force**.
It is one of the highest geyser fields on the planet.
Visitors arrive before sunrise, when the cold air makes the steam easier to see.
At that hour, the temperature can be several degrees below zero.
[pause]

# A natural laboratory
@energy 4
> Newscast close: calm and firm.

To finish, the Atacama as a laboratory for understanding other worlds.
Its dry, salty soil is similar, in some ways, to the soil of **Mars**.
That's why various scientific teams have tested robots and exploration equipment there.
Researchers also study bacteria that can live with very little water.
In certain areas, the ground is so dry that there is almost no microbial life on the surface.
What we learn in the desert helps design future **space** missions.
Harsh, high and silent, the Atacama keeps surprising those who visit it.
And for the people who live there, it is simply **home**.
That concludes our special report.
