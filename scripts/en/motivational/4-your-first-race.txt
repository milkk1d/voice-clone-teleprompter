# The couch
@energy 3
> Start calm, with gentle humor.

A year ago, my **favorite** sport was watching other people play sports.
From the couch, with a bowl of chips in my hand.
Climbing two flights of stairs left me out of breath.
Running seemed like something for **others**.
For people with **long** legs and huge lungs.
Sound familiar? [up]
One day, someone invited me to a five-kilometer **race**.
I laughed.
I said I'd think about it, which was the same as saying no.
Five kilometers seemed like an impossible **distance** to me.
[pause 2s]
But something about that invitation kept **turning** in my head.
What if...
What if I could do it too? [up]
That question changed **everything**.

# The first minute
@energy 4
> More energy, with honesty.

On the first day, I went **jogging** at dawn and lasted one minute.
One minute.
I came home with a red face and my **pride** on the floor.
But the next day I went out again.
With sore legs and the same old sneakers.
I jogged one minute, walked two.
I jogged one minute, walked **two**.
[pause 2s]
I did that for weeks, without **anyone** seeing me.
Little by little, one **minute** became two, and two became five.
It wasn't fast.
Dogs, kids on bikes and even a lady with grocery bags passed me.
It wasn't **pretty**.
But it was **mine**!
And every time I came home, I felt a **little** stronger.

# Race day
@energy 4
> Growing excitement.

Three months later, race **day** arrived.
There were **hundreds** of people, music and colorful balloons.
My legs were shaking at the starting line.
I thought about going home.
Nobody would have noticed, and nobody would have judged me.
[pause 2s]
But the starting **gun** went off, and I started running.
The first kilometer was **easy**.
The second, a little less.
In the third, I wanted to **stop**.
Then I heard a little girl at the side of the road shouting: "Come on, you can do it!"
She didn't know me, but I **believed** every word.
And I kept going.

# The finish line
@energy 4
> The most emotional moment.

The last kilometer was the **longest** of my life.
My legs hurt, my lungs hurt, even my will to **keep** going hurt.
But in the distance, I saw the **finish** arch.
[pause 2s]
And I crossed it.
With my arms up and a smile too big for my face.
Thirty-eight minutes.
Not fast, not slow: my own time.
Nobody gave me a gold **medal**.
They gave me a **copper** one, like everyone else.
But I hung it on the wall as if it were the most **important** prize in the world.
Because it **was**.
It's still my favorite treasure.
That medal didn't say "you won the race."
It said: "you beat the **couch**."

# Your first race
@energy 5
> The close: full energy.

Now it's **your** turn.
It doesn't matter if today you can't run for even a **minute**.
I couldn't either.
Nobody starts out fast; everyone starts by starting.
Start walking, jogging a little, walking again.
It's not easy. It's not fast. But it's **possible**.
[pause 2s]
Find a race in the next three **months**.
Write it on your calendar.
And go out today, even for ten **minutes**.
Your body is **stronger** than you think.
What will your first goal be? [up]
See you at the **start**!
[loud]From the couch to the finish line, one **step** at a time![/loud]
