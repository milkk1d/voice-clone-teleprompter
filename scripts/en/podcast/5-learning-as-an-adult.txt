# The hard weeks

> More honest, without drama.

The first months were harder than I'd imagined.
My fingers wouldn't obey, and my left hand seemed to belong to someone else.
I practiced half an hour every night after work, with headphones so I wouldn't bother anyone.
Some weeks I made no progress at **all**.
And then the voice came back: "See? It's too late."
[pause]
I set myself one rule: never miss a class.
And I discovered something important: learning as an adult has its advantages.

Plus, you make mistakes without being so afraid of looking silly.

# My first song

After eight months, I could play my first complete song.
It was a lullaby my grandma used to sing to help me fall asleep.
I played it on her birthday, on the old piano at my aunt's house.
I made three mistakes, and one of them was pretty **obvious**.
[pause]
But when I finished, I saw my grandma's eyes were full of tears.
"My mother used to play that song," she told me in a low voice.

It was the best concert of my life, a pure pleasure, with an audience of five!
That day I understood it's never too late to learn something that **matters** to you.
