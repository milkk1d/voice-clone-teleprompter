Today I want to talk about someone who changed my life: my first **dog**.
His name was Tango, and he was the messiest dog in the neighborhood.
He arrived at our house on a rainy afternoon, inside a shoebox.
Well, it was a boot box, because he was already pretty big.
He had giant paws, one ear that stood up and one that flopped down.
My dad said we'd only keep him for a few days, until we found him a family.
Can you guess how many years he stayed? [up]
Fourteen.
Honestly, no one in the house ever thought about giving him **back**.

# The first days
@energy 3
> With humor.

The first days with Tango were total **chaos**.
He ate a shoe, two socks and half a cushion from the living room.
He barked at every shadow in the yard.
He cried all night if we left him in the kitchen, next to his bowl.
So I ended up sleeping on the floor, against every rule in the house.
I spent the night beside him, with a blanket and a flashlight, shivering with cold.
My mom found me there at seven in the morning and nearly fainted.
I had dog hair even on my eyelashes!
Look, at nine years old I thought having a dog meant playing all day.
Nobody had told me about the other part: cleaning, walking and lots of **patience**.

# What he taught me
@energy 3
> More reflective.

Over the years, I realized Tango was teaching me **important** things.
The first one was responsibility.
If I didn't take him for a walk, nobody did.
He would wait for me by the door with the leash in his mouth.
The second was learning to read what someone feels without words.
I knew when he was hungry, when he was scared and when he just wanted company.
And the third, the hardest one, was to **forgive** fast.
[pause]
Because a dog doesn't hold a grudge, even if you come home late or forget to play with him.
He greets you wagging his tail, as if you were the best person in the world.
To be honest, I'm still learning that lesson.

# Saying goodbye
@energy 4
> The most emotional moment: slow and sincere.

Tango lived for fourteen years, and he spent the last ones sleeping in the sun on the patio.
By then he walked slowly and his muzzle had turned white.
One autumn afternoon, he lay down in his favorite corner and didn't get up again.
[pause]
I'll confess I cried like I'd never cried before.
For weeks, I kept looking at the door whenever I heard a noise.
But one day, while cleaning out the garage, I found his old boot box.
Inside were his first collar, a chewed-up toy and a photo of the two of us, a real treasure.
And instead of sadness, I felt something different: **gratitude**.
What luck I had to grow up with him!
