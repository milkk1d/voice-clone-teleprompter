# Recipes without measurements
@energy 3
> With humor, remembering.

The first thing I learned is that my grandma never used **measurements**.
No cups, no grams, no teaspoons.
Everything was "a handful," "a splash" or "whatever the dough asks for."
And at ten years old, I would ask her: "And how much is the dough asking for?"
She would laugh and say: "You'll learn to listen to it."
Honestly, back then that answer sounded **impossible**.
Once I tried to make her cookies at home, following my own calculations.
They came out so hard that my brother used one as a coaster!
To this day, my family tells that story at every birthday.
But that's when I understood something: cooking isn't just following steps, it's paying attention.

# The cake disaster

> A quick pace: it's the funniest story.

Now let me tell you about the most famous **disaster** in my family.
For my grandma's seventieth birthday, I wanted to surprise her with a cake.
I got up before dawn to start.
It was my first three-layer cake, with cream, strawberries and chocolate glaze.
I put it together in her kitchen, in secret, while she was taking a nap.
Everything was perfect until I opened the oven and saw the sponge had **sunk** in the middle.
It looked like a crater, I'm not kidding.
[pause]
I panicked and started filling the hole with cream, fruit and chocolate.
When my grandma woke up, she found me with flour in my hair and cream on my ears.
And do you know what she did? [up]
She tasted the cake, closed her eyes and said it was the best one she'd ever had.

# What I learned

> The most emotional moment: slower and warmer.

A couple of years ago, my grandma moved to a smaller house near my aunt.
She doesn't cook as much anymore, because her hands hurt.
Now I'm the one who makes Sunday lunch.

What I learned in that kitchen wasn't recipes, it was **patience**.

And I learned that food is a way of saying "I love you" without words.
