# The driest place
@energy 4
> Confidence and clarity: the facts speak for themselves.

The Atacama Desert lies in the north of Chile.
It is the **driest** non-polar desert on the planet.
In some areas, not a single drop of rain has been recorded for years.
To the east, the Andes mountains block the clouds.
To the west, a cold current from the Pacific keeps rain from forming.
So the region is trapped between two **barriers**.
By day, the sun can burn your skin in minutes.
At night, the temperature can drop close to zero.
[breathe]
Among stones and sand, the soil is so dry that bacteria can hardly survive.
There are areas where not a single plant grows!
But the Atacama holds **surprises**.

# Water from the fog
@energy 4
> Curiosity: tell it like a clever trick.

If it doesn't rain, where do plants and people get water? [up]
The answer comes from the sea, in the form of fog.
On the coast, that thick fog has a name: **camanchaca{ca-man-CHA-ca}**.
Some villages set up large nets to catch it.
They're called **fog catchers**.
Droplets hit the net, join together and run into a channel.
Each net can collect many liters of water a day.
[pause]
With that water, people grow gardens and even brew beer.
It's a simple, cheap system that uses no **electricity**.
Other deserts around the world have copied the idea.
That way, a village with almost no rain can have its own garden.

# The best sky in the world
@energy 4
> Wonder: here your voice opens up, like the sky.

With no clouds or moisture, the Atacama sky is one of the **clearest** on Earth.
It has more than three hundred clear nights a year.
That's why some of the largest observatories in the world were built there.
One of them uses sixty-six antennas that work together.
They sit more than five thousand meters above sea level.
[breathe]
From there, scientists study distant galaxies and the birth of **stars**.
At night, with the naked eye, the Milky Way looks like a white cloud.
Some say it's like seeing the universe with new eyes.
It's a vision many travelers come looking for!
And there's more: when it rains hard, the desert **fills** with flowers.
It happens every few years and lasts a few weeks.

# A piece of Mars
@energy 4
> Intriguing tone, almost science fiction.

Its reddish, dry, mineral-rich soil looks a lot like the soil of **Mars**.
That's why space agencies test their exploring robots there.
If a machine works in the Atacama, it has a better chance on another planet.
Science fiction movies have been filmed there too.
[pause]
Scientists also search for **microbes** that survive in those conditions.
If they find life here, we might learn where to look for it out there.
Some corners look as empty as the surface of the red planet.
The desert also hides huge salt flats that shine so white they're dazzling.
Beneath them lies **lithium**, a key metal for batteries.
The same place helps us study the universe and charge your phone.
It's a place that breaks every rule: extreme and useful at the same time.
