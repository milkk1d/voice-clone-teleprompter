# The brain's alarm
@energy 4
> Explain with confidence, like a good teacher.

Your brain is designed to notice **everything** new.
A strange place, new faces and rules you don't understand yet.
For a part of the brain called the amygdala, that can look like a **threat**.
So it switches on a very old response: get ready to act.
[pause]
The adrenal glands release **adrenaline** and cortisol.
Your heart beats faster and your breathing speeds up.
Your muscles tense up, ready to move.
Everything feels more intense, faster and more important.
That's why the first day seems to last twice as long.
It's the same system that helped our ancestors escape from predators.
Except today, the predator is a team meeting!

# Butterflies in your stomach
@energy 4
> Friendly tone, with a touch of humor.

Do you know that feeling of butterflies in your stomach? [up]
It has a fairly simple **physical** explanation.
When your body gets ready to act, it sends less blood to your digestion.
That blood goes to your muscles and your brain.
Your stomach **notices**, and that strange tingling appears.
Some people feel like going to the bathroom every few minutes.
It's the same reaction, just in another part of the body.
[breathe]
Your hands may sweat, or your mouth may get dry.
It's not a **mistake** in your body: it's your body getting ready.
And the most interesting part is that this feeling is very close to excitement.
On the inside, nerves and joy feel almost the same!

# First impressions
@energy 4
> Surprising fact: give it a pause first.

There's another reason for nerves: we want to make a **good** impression.
Plus, we spend days imagining how it will all go.
And the mind usually imagines the worst.
Some studies show we judge a new face in [pause] a **tenth** of a second.
In that instant, we decide whether someone seems trustworthy, friendly or confident.
But that first impression isn't final.
As days go by, what you do matters more than that first look.
Arriving on time, listening and asking questions build your image.
[breathe]
Nobody expects you to know everything on day one.
What people **remember** is your attitude.

# How to calm down
@energy 4
> Practical and kind, like advice from a friend.

The good news is that you can **turn** down the volume of that alarm.
Breathing slowly, letting the air out longer than you take it in, calms the body.
You can try it right now: breathe in for four counts and out for six.
After a few breaths, your heart starts to slow down.
That kind of breathing switches on the system that brakes the stress response.
[pause]
Another trick is to change the words you tell yourself.
Instead of "I'm nervous," try "I'm **excited**."
Several experiments show that this phrase improves performance.
It also helps to lay out your clothes, your route and your bag the night before.
Fewer decisions in the morning mean **less** tension.
And remember: everyone who welcomes you today also had a first day once.
