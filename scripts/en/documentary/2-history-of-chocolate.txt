# Born in the rainforest
@energy 4
> Explain with confidence, like someone unfolding a map.

Cacao comes from a tree that grows in the Amazon rainforest.
More than five thousand years ago, people were already using it in what is now **Ecuador**.
From there, its cultivation traveled north to Mexico and Central America.
The Olmecs, the Maya and the Aztecs turned it into something **sacred**.
The Maya even had a god linked to cacao.
Their pottery shows cacao trees and people drinking it.
But they didn't eat it in bars; they drank it.
It was a mix of ground cacao, water and spices, sometimes with chili.
It was foamy, cold and **strong**.
[breathe]
Above all, the elite and the warriors drank it.
Nothing like the chocolate we know today!

# Money you could drink
@energy 4
> Surprise: this fact hooks people.

For the Aztecs, cacao seeds had another [pause] very curious use.
They worked as **money**.
With a few seeds, you could buy a tomato or a tamale.
With about a hundred, you could get a **turkey**.
Imagine paying at the market with your own breakfast.
It was like having bills that grew on trees.
Some people even made fake seeds out of mud.
That's why merchants checked each seed with great care.
[breathe]
Cacao was so valuable that people stored it like **treasure**.
Can you imagine keeping chocolate in a safe? [up]
For them, it made perfect sense.

# The journey to Europe
@energy 4
> An adventure rhythm, with clear facts.

In the sixteenth century, cacao crossed the ocean to Spain.
There, they added **sugar** and cinnamon and served it hot.
At first, many Europeans found it far too bitter.
But in Madrid, the drink became the fashion at the royal court.
It was so popular that people argued whether it broke the religious fast.
For almost three hundred years, chocolate was still something you drank from a cup.
[pause]
Only in eighteen forty-seven did the first **bar** for eating appear.
A few decades later, in Switzerland, someone created milk **chocolate**.
New machines made its texture smooth and creamy.
The luxury of kings began to reach every corner shop.
Today you find it in bars, bonbons, ice cream and even in savory dishes.

# Cacao today
@energy 4
> Firm tone: these are facts few people know.

Although it was born in America, today cacao is grown above all in **Africa**.
Ivory Coast and Ghana produce more than half of the world's cacao.
Millions of farming families make a living from it.
The tree needs heat, rain and shade, close to the equator.
Its pods grow stuck to the trunk, like colorful ears of corn.
Inside each pod there are about forty seeds wrapped in **white** pulp.
[pause]
Those seeds are fermented, dried and roasted.
Only then does the aroma we all recognize appear.
Its scientific name, Theobroma{the-o-BRO-ma}, means "food of the **gods**".
And what a good name it is!
