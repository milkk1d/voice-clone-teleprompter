# Before you record
@energy 3
> A patient, practical teacher's tone.

Today I'll show you how to record your voice at home with good quality, without a studio.
It works for a podcast, for your videos or for cloning your voice with artificial intelligence.
What matters most isn't an expensive microphone, but the **room**.
A good room with a simple microphone sounds better than a great microphone in a kitchen.
You'll need a microphone, headphones and a free program like Audacity.
Do you have everything ready? [up]
Then let's start with the **space**.
There are just three steps and a **final** review.

# Step one: the room
@energy 3
> Clear and concrete.

First, find the **quietest** spot in your home, with the least echo.
Even in a small home, there's always a corner that works.
A closet full of clothes or a bedroom with thick curtains works very well.
Fabric absorbs sound; smooth walls and glass make it bounce.
Clap once: if you hear a long echo, that room isn't ready yet.
You can improve it with blankets, pillows or a mattress against the wall.
Turn off the fridge, the fan and your phone notifications.
[loud]Watch out for noises you no longer notice, like a clock or traffic.[/loud]
Record at night or early in the morning, when the street is **quieter**.
[pause]

# Step two: the microphone
@energy 3
> Patient and precise.

Second, place the microphone about a **hand** away from your mouth, around fifteen centimeters.
If it's too close, the letters p and b hit it and sound like small explosions.
To avoid that, put it a little to the side, not right in front of your mouth.
A pop filter, or even a stocking stretched over a hoop, also **helps**.
The golden rule: always keep the same distance.
Why does it matter so much? [up]
Because if you move closer and farther away, your voice changes in **volume** and tone.
Consistency is the key to a professional recording!
[pause]

# Step three: recording in Audacity
@energy 3
> A steady rhythm.

Third, open Audacity and choose your microphone in the toolbar.
Set the recording to **mono**, because a single voice doesn't need stereo.
Before you begin, say a few test sentences and watch the meter that measures your volume.
Record one test minute and listen to it with headphones.
The bars should move around the middle, never reaching the **red**.
If they hit red, lower the gain; if they hardly move, raise it.
At the start, leave two seconds of silence, so the program learns how your room sounds.
[loud]Careful: don't speak while you click, because that sound gets recorded too.[/loud]
Read with calm, and if you make a mistake, pause and repeat the **whole** sentence.
[pause]

# Summary
@energy 3
> A friendly close.

Let's review the most important points.
First, the room: quiet, with fabric and no echo.
Second, the microphone: a hand away, a little to the side and always at the same **distance**.
Third, Audacity: in mono, with the level at mid-bar and two seconds of silence at the start.
Drink **warm** water before recording and take breaks every fifteen minutes.
With this, you can record like you're in a **studio**!
And if you want scripts made for your voice, you'll find them on this site.
Now, let's record.
