# Before you start
@energy 3
> A warm, excited tone.

Would you like to eat tomatoes or lettuce grown on your own **balcony**? [up]
Today I'll show you how to set up a small garden, even with little space.
The most important thing is **light**: vegetables need at least five hours of sun a day.
Watch your balcony for a day and note when it gets sun.
You can use your phone's compass to see which way it faces.
If it gets little light, choose herbs like mint, parsley or cilantro.
If it gets a lot, you can plant tomatoes, peppers and strawberries.
You don't need experience: just **enthusiasm** and a little patience.
People have grown their own food for centuries, and so can you.

# Step one: pots and soil
@energy 3
> Clear and practical.

First, choose **pots** with holes in the bottom.
Without holes, water builds up and the roots rot.
For lettuce and herbs, pots twenty centimeters deep are enough.
Tomatoes need more space: at least forty centimeters.
Then fill them with potting mix, which is **looser** and richer.
[loud]Careful: don't use soil from the yard, because it packs down and brings pests.[/loud]
Mix in a handful of **compost** to give the plants more food.
Leave two fingers of space between the soil and the rim.
[pause]

# Step two: planting
@energy 3
> Patient, step by step.

Second, decide whether to sow seeds or use small plants from a nursery.
Seeds are cheaper, but they take longer to grow.
Nursery plants save you **weeks** and are ideal for beginners.
Dig a hole the size of the roots, place the plant and press the soil gently.
If you use seeds, bury them twice as **deep** as their size.
How many plants per pot? [up]
As a rule, one tomato, or three or four lettuces.
Don't crowd them, they need room to **breathe**!
[pause]

# Step three: watering and care
@energy 3
> Firm and kind.

Third, learn to **water** well, because that's where most people go wrong.
Water early in the morning or at sunset, never in full sun.
Push a **finger** into the soil: if it's dry down to the first knuckle, it's time to water.
In summer, maybe every day; in winter, once or twice a week.
[loud]Careful with overwatering: too much water is worse than thirst.[/loud]
Check the leaves every few days, in case spots or insects appear.
The yellow flowers on your tomato plant mean the fruit is coming soon.
If you see aphids, a little water with mild soap usually does the trick.
And turn the pots from time to time, so they grow **straight**.
[pause]

First, check how much **light** it gets and choose what to plant.
Second, prepare pots with good drainage and proper potting mix.
