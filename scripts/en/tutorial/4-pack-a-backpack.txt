# Before you pack
@energy 3
> A practical, upbeat tone.

Have you ever carried a full backpack and used only half of it? [up]
Today I'll teach you to pack a travel backpack that's light, tidy and **practical**.
The **golden** rule is simple: if in doubt, leave it out.
For a one-week trip, a forty-liter backpack is more than enough.
Before you start, spread everything you want to bring on the bed.
That way, you'll see at a glance what's extra.
Then sort it all into three piles: essential, useful and just in case.
And remember: every extra kilo ends up on your **back**.
Let's go step by step.

# Step one: clothes
@energy 3
> Clear and concrete.

First, choose your **clothes** thinking in outfits, not single pieces.
Bring colors that match each other, so a few pieces make several outfits.
For one week, three T-shirts, two pairs of pants and a light jacket are enough.
Add underwear and socks for four days: you can always do laundry.
Instead of folding, **roll** your clothes: they take up less space and wrinkle less.
A large scarf works as a shawl, a towel or a picnic blanket.
[loud]Careful with an extra pair of shoes: they weigh a lot and rarely get used.[/loud]
Travel wearing your **heaviest** shoes, not packing them.
[pause]

# Step two: order
@energy 3
> Patient, step by step.

Second, think about **order**, because a well-packed backpack feels lighter.
At the bottom go the things you won't use during the day, like a sleeping bag.
In the middle, close to your back, go the **heaviest** items.
That way the weight stays close to your body and doesn't pull you backward.
On top, what you need at hand: a jacket for the cold, water and a snack.
In the **small** pockets, keep documents, a charger and sunscreen.
Why so much order? [up]
Because then you don't have to empty the whole backpack to find one sock.
[pause]

# Step three: the essentials
@energy 3
> Firm and kind.

Third, put together a small **first-aid** kit with the basics.
Bandages, painkillers, something for your stomach and your usual medicine.
Keep a digital **copy** of your passport and bookings on your phone.
Bring a foldable cloth bag for shopping or dirty clothes.
A small padlock will give you peace of mind in hostels.
And a portable charger saves the day on long bus rides.
[loud]Careful with liquids: on planes, only small containers are allowed.[/loud]
Last of all, lift the backpack and walk around the house for **ten** minutes.
If it bothers you, you know what to do: take something out.
[pause]

# Summary
@energy 3
> An upbeat close.

Let's review how to pack your travel backpack.
First, clothes that match, rolled up and in small amounts.
Second, heavy things in the middle and urgent things on top.
Third, a first-aid kit, copies of your documents and a **weight** test at home.
Traveling light gives you freedom: you can move, change plans and enjoy.
Your back will **thank** you!
Where will you take your backpack first? [up]
Have a great trip, and may you never carry **anything** extra!
