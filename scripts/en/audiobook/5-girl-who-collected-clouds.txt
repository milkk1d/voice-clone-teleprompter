# The jars
@energy 2
> A bedtime story voice, soft and warm.

In a village of yellow hills lived a girl named Amalia.
She had a collection that no one else in the world had: she kept **clouds**.
She caught them at dawn, when they came down to rest on the meadow.
She used glass jars, the kind her grandmother filled with jam.
She had round clouds, long clouds, a giant one and a tiny one shaped like a rabbit.
Each jar was a small treasure.
Every night she lined them up on the shelf by her window.
[soft]Sometimes, in the silence, one of them let three drops fall on the glass.[/soft]
Her grandmother was the only one who never laughed at her.
Her older brother, on the other hand, teased her all the time.
"What good is a cloud in a jar?" he would ask.
Amalia just shrugged.
"I don't know yet. But **someday** they'll be useful."

# The drought
@energy 3
> The tension builds, without hurry.

That summer, it didn't rain a single time.
The river shrank to a trickle, and the cows looked for shade under the dry trees.
Even the birds stopped singing in the afternoons.
The grown-ups looked at the sky every morning, their foreheads **wrinkled** with worry.
In the square, in front of the church, the mayor announced that the village well was almost empty.
[pause 2s]
That night, Amalia couldn't sleep.
Her brother wasn't teasing anymore: he was thirsty too.
She looked at her jars one by one, lit by the moon.
The clouds were stirring, as if they were thirsty as well.
Then she understood **why** she had kept them for so long.
She put on her sneakers without a sound and slipped out of the house with the jars in her arms.

# The rain
@energy 4
> The climax: growing joy.

She climbed the highest hill in the village in the middle of the night.
She set the jars on the grass and took a deep breath.
Then she opened them one by one, very carefully.
For a moment, the clouds hesitated, as if they were afraid to leave her.
Then they drifted out, stretching like cats after a nap.
They rose, gathered and grew until they covered the **whole** sky.
[pause 2s]
The first drop landed on her nose.
Then came another, and another, and suddenly it was raining over the whole valley.
The neighbors came out of their houses in pajamas, laughing and looking up.
"It's raining! It's finally raining!" her brother shouted, soaking wet.
Amalia raised her arms and let the water run down her **face**.

# The last jar
@energy 2
> End very slowly, like the end of a fairy tale.

It rained for three days straight.
The river sang again, the well filled up and the fields turned green once more.
The cows went back to the fields, and the mayor gave a speech that nobody listened to.
No one could explain where those clouds had come from.
Well, almost no one.
Her brother gave her a new jar, the biggest one he could find in the kitchen.
"So you can start a new collection," he said, a little embarrassed.
Amalia put it on the shelf, empty and shining.
[soft]Every morning at dawn, she looks at it with patience, waiting for the **right** cloud.[/soft]
They say that if you watch that window with care, you'll sometimes see her smile.
And in that village, ever since, no one laughs at a little girl's **dreams**. [down]
