# The discovery
@energy 2
> Narrate calmly.

Julian had been working as the town's mail carrier for only a month.
That week, the post office was being renovated, and the old furniture had to be moved.
When they pushed a heavy cabinet aside, an envelope covered in dust fell to the floor.
It had an old stamp and a date from **forty** years ago.
It was addressed to Carmen Ibanez, fourteen Orange Tree Street.
"It must have been stuck behind the cabinet," said the head of the office.
Julian looked at the envelope for a long time.
No one had opened it, and the handwriting was **careful**, almost shy.
Could that woman still be living there? [up]
That afternoon, after finishing his route, he decided to find out.

# The search
@energy 3
> Curiosity grows.

At number fourteen on Orange Tree Street, there was now a bakery.
The owner didn't know any Carmen, but she called her mother on the phone.
"Of course! Carmen lives in the blue house next to the church," came the answer.
Julian crossed the square with the envelope in his hand and his heart racing.
People in the neighborhood waved at him without knowing what he was carrying.
The door was opened by a woman with white hair, wearing an apron covered in flour.
[pause 2s]
"Carmen Ibanez?" Julian asked.
"That's me. Is something wrong?"
Julian explained the story and handed her the envelope.
Carmen read the sender's **name** and stood very still.
It was Martin, the young man who had gone to work in the south when she was twenty.
She had never heard from **him** again.

# The letter
@energy 4
> The climax: restrained emotion, no exaggeration.

Carmen sat down in the kitchen and opened the envelope with care.
She read it aloud, her voice breaking.
"Carmen: the work in the south is hard, but I've saved enough for a small house."
"If you still love me, wait for me at the station on the first day of spring."
[pause 2s]
Carmen set the letter on the table.
"I thought he had forgotten me," she whispered. "I waited a whole year."
"And he must have thought I didn't **want** to wait."
Julian didn't know what to say.
At the bottom of the letter was an address in a city in the south.
"Maybe he still lives there!" Julian said all of a sudden.
Carmen looked at him with **shining** eyes.
"Then help me write to him!"

# The reply
@energy 2
> End tenderly.

That night they wrote a short letter together, with no frills.
Carmen told him about her life: a happy marriage, two children, a garden and the calm of her seventies.
At the end, she added a single sentence: "I got your letter. It arrived forty years late."
Julian took it to the office himself the next day.
Three weeks went by.
[soft]Every morning, Carmen waited for the mail carrier at her door, with a cup of coffee for him.[/soft]
One Monday, Julian came running with an envelope in his hand.
It had the **same** careful handwriting, now a little shaky.
Carmen didn't open it at the door.
She tucked it into her apron pocket like a treasure and smiled.
"I'll read this one alone," she said. "But tomorrow I'll tell you **everything**." [down]
