# Before dawn
@energy 2
> A warm tone, with a little humor.

Julia's alarm went off at four in the morning.
It was her first day at Ramon's bakery, the oldest one in the neighborhood.
She got dressed in the dark, half asleep and full of nerves.
On the way, she went over everything she had learned in class: flour, water, salt and patience.
The bakery smelled of firewood and fresh bread.
Ramon was waiting at the door, in a white apron and with a **serious** face.
"There's only one rule here: you don't have to be fast, you have to be **steady**," he said.
He showed her the ovens, the wooden tables and a notebook full of handwritten recipes.
"Today you'll make the bread rolls for the school party," he added.
Julia swallowed hard.
It was **three hundred** rolls.

# The mistake
@energy 3
> The tension rises.

Julia kneaded dough for hours, her arms more tired by the minute.
At seven, she slid the first trays into the oven and sat down to rest for a minute.
Just one minute.
[pause 2s]
When she opened her eyes, a burning smell filled the whole kitchen.
"No, no, no!" she shouted, running to the oven.
The rolls were as **black** as coal.
Ramon walked in at that moment and looked at the trays without a word.
Julia felt her face burning with shame.
"I'm so sorry. If you want, I'll leave right now," she said under her breath.
The baker crossed his arms and stared at her for a **long** time.
There were barely two hours left until the delivery.

# Against the clock
@energy 4
> The climax: a quick pace and joy at the end.

"Leave? Don't even think about it," Ramon answered, rolling up his sleeves.
"You knead, I shape, and the oven is never left **alone**, not even for a second."
They worked without talking, at a pace Julia didn't think was possible.
She weighed the flour, and he shaped the rolls with quick, precise movements.
Each tray that came out of the oven was more golden than the last.
[pause 2s]
At nine o'clock sharp, the school van arrived.
Three hundred rolls, warm and perfect, waited in baskets on the counter.
"We did it!" Julia cried, with flour even on her eyebrows.
The school principal tasted one and closed her eyes with pleasure.
"This is the best bread in the **neighborhood**," she said.
Ramon winked at Julia and didn't correct **anyone**.

# The notebook
@energy 2
> Intimate and funny. End slowly.

At the end of the day, Ramon poured her a coffee and opened his old notebook.
On the first page there was a **dark** stain and a date from forty years ago.
He asked her, with half a smile: "Do you know what this is?" [up]
Julia shook her head.
[soft]"My first day. I burned all the bread in town."[/soft]
She laughed so hard she almost spilled her coffee.
"The mistake doesn't matter that much," he went on. "What matters is what you do **next**."
Since that day, Julia arrives every morning at four, without an alarm.
She has her own page in the notebook now, with her own recipe.
And in one corner, she drew a small burnt roll.
She says it's so she never forgets her first **day**. [down]
