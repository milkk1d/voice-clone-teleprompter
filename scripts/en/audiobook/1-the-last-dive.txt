# The descent
@energy 2
> Narrate calmly, like opening a book at night.

Marina had been piloting the small research submarine for twelve years.
That morning, the sea was still and the sky was a pearly gray.
From the ship, Julio checked the oxygen, the lights and the radio one last time.
"This is your last dive before you retire," he said with a smile.
"Then let's make it **unforgettable**," she answered.
The hatch closed with a heavy thud.
The submarine began to sink, and the blue grew darker and darker.
At two hundred meters, the sunlight had almost disappeared.
At a thousand, there was only **blackness** and the hum of the engines.
Marina switched on the floodlights and watched white specks drift by, like snow.
[soft]She loved that silence more than anything in the world.[/soft]

# The blackout
@energy 3
> Don't dramatize yet: the tension builds slowly.

At two thousand meters, an alarm broke the calm.
The lights flickered and, all at once, **everything** went dark.
"Julio, I've lost main power," Marina said, without raising her voice.
The radio crackled for a few endless seconds.
"I hear you. How much oxygen do you have left?" [up]
"About six hours," she replied.
It was enough to surface, but without the engines she couldn't move.
The submarine floated motionless, hanging in the dark like a stone in the air.
[pause 2s]
Then she saw something that **wasn't** in any manual.
A blue light switched on in front of the window, then another, and another.
They were jellyfish, lanternfish and tiny squid, all glowing to the same **rhythm**.

# The dance of lights
@energy 4
> The climax: more energy, but no shouting.

"Julio, you have to see this!" she cried, forgetting her fear.
The lights spun around the submarine, forming a perfect **circle**.
Then they all moved upward, as if they were showing her the way.
Marina remembered a rule her mother taught her: when you don't know what to do, watch.
She released the emergency weights, and the submarine began to rise.
The lights went with her, climbing beside her meter by meter.
"You're doing great, keep going!" Julio repeated from the surface, his voice breaking.
[pause 2s]
At five hundred meters, the animals switched off **all at once**.
Maybe they didn't need her anymore, or maybe she didn't need them.
Above her, the water turned gray, then green, then a **brilliant** blue.

# Back on deck
@energy 2
> The calm returns. End slowly.

When she opened the hatch, the cold air hit her face.
Julio helped her out onto the platform and hugged her without a word.
After a while, he asked in a low voice: "What happened down there?" [up]
Marina looked at the sea for a long time.
[soft]"They brought me back," she whispered.[/soft]
No one on the ship fully understood that answer.
That night she wrote the dive report with exact data: depth, time and temperature.
But she kept one page **separate**, just for herself.
On that page, she drew a circle of blue lights, her private treasure.
Years later, people at the harbor still tell the story of the pilot the deep sea guided home.
And whenever she hears it, she smiles and doesn't **correct** a thing. [down]
