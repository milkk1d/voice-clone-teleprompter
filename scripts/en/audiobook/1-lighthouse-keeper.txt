# The island
@energy 2
> Narrate calmly, like telling a story by the fire.

Elvira had looked after the lighthouse on Crow Island for thirty years.
She lived alone, with a gray cat, an old radio and a library of worn-out books.
Every evening she climbed the hundred and twelve steps of the tower to switch on the light.
She had only one rule: the lighthouse light never goes out.
[soft]She used to say the sea spoke to her, and that you had to know how to listen.[/soft]
That autumn evening, the sky turned a **strange** color, somewhere between green and purple.
The gulls flew low, and the wind carried the smell of rain.
Elvira closed the windows and checked the generator twice.
"It's going to be a long night," she told the cat.
The cat didn't answer, but it hid under the bed.
Down below, the waves began to pound the rocks and the beach with real **force**.

# The storm
@energy 3
> The tension builds.

At midnight, the storm hit the island with all its fury.
The rain beat against the windows as if someone were trying to get in.
All at once, the radio crackled and a desperate voice filled the kitchen.
"This is the fishing boat Southern Star. We can't see a thing, we're near the rocks!"
Elvira ran up the tower.
[pause 2s]
The lighthouse was **dark**.
Lightning had damaged the generator, and the great lamp wouldn't respond.
Out among the waves, a tiny red light was drifting toward the rocks.
That boat was heading straight for the **reef**.
How could she guide them without a light? [up]

# The light
@energy 4
> The climax: firmness and urgency.

Then she remembered the oil lamp her grandfather had kept in the cellar.
She went down to find it, wiped it with her sleeve and lit it with trembling hands.
She climbed the hundred and twelve steps again, soaked and out of breath.
She set the lamp in front of the great mirror and began to turn it by hand.
It was heavy, but it moved.
A **faint** beam of light swept out to sea, then another, and another.
[pause 2s]
The tiny red light hesitated for a moment, then changed course.
"I see you! You're turning!" Elvira shouted, though no one could hear her.
She kept turning the mirror all night long, until her arms ached.
At dawn, the fishing boat sailed into the harbor on the other side of the island, **safe** and sound.

# The visit
@energy 2
> Calm after the storm. End tenderly.

Two days later, a small boat pulled up to the island's dock.
It was Simon, the captain of the fishing boat, with his daughter and a basket of bread, cheese and fruit.
"You saved our lives," he said, taking off his cap.
Elvira smiled and showed him the old oil lamp, her new treasure.
"To be honest, it was my grandfather who saved you."
In the afternoon they drank tea in the kitchen while the cat slept by the **stove**.
The captain's daughter asked if she could climb the tower.
[soft]From the top, the sea looked calm, as if it had never been angry at all.[/soft]
Before leaving, Simon promised to come back every autumn.
And he kept his word: every year, when the sky turns strange, a boat arrives with **fresh** bread.
Elvira waits for it on the dock, with the oil lamp still **burning**. [down]
