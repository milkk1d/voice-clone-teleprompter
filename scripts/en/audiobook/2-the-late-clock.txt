# The workshop
@energy 2
> Narrate calmly, with a touch of mystery.

Don Aurelio had a clock shop on a narrow street downtown.
The walls were covered with clocks of every size, and all of them showed the same time.
People in the neighborhood said time never made mistakes in there.
That steady ticking was, to him, the most **beautiful** music in the world.
One rainy morning, a young woman named Ines came in with a wooden box under her arm.
Inside was a table clock, golden, with flowers painted on its face.
"It was my grandmother's," she explained. "It works, but it always runs seven minutes slow."
Don Aurelio examined it through his magnifying glass.
It had dust, a tired spring and a small crack in the glass.
"Leave it with me for a week. I'll make it **perfect**," he promised.
Ines hesitated before letting go of the box.
[soft]It was as if she were saying goodbye to someone.[/soft]

# The problem
@energy 3
> The tension builds: something doesn't make sense.

Don Aurelio cleaned every piece, changed the spring and adjusted the wheels one by one.
He left it running all night beside the most accurate clock in the shop.
The next morning, Ines's clock was seven minutes slow.
"Impossible!" the clockmaker muttered, taking off his glasses.
He repeated the work twice more, with the same **result**.
It wasn't six minutes, or eight: it was always exactly seven.
He no longer knew what else to check.
[pause 2s]
Then he noticed something he hadn't seen before.
On the back, hidden under a brass plate, there was a tiny **door**.
What could a clock be hiding in a place like that? [up]
With a fine needle, he opened it very slowly.

# The secret
@energy 4
> The climax: restrained emotion.

Inside was a small treasure: a folded piece of paper, yellow with age.
He sat down at his workbench and unfolded it with care.
It was a handwritten note, in elegant writing and almost faded ink.
"Rosa: I set this clock back so I could have seven more minutes with you every morning."
Below it was a signature and a date from sixty years earlier.
All at once, Don Aurelio understood.
Ines's grandfather had set the clock slow **on purpose**.
[pause 2s]
Every morning, the clock told them it was still early, and they ate breakfast together without hurrying.
He arrived at work seven minutes late, but he gained seven minutes with her.
"What a **strange** deal, and what a beautiful one!" he said out loud, alone in the shop.

# The decision
@energy 2
> Intimate and warm. End slowly.

A week later, Ines came back to the shop.
Don Aurelio handed her the clock, clean and shiny, with new glass.
"I fixed it," he said. "But first, I want to show you something."
He gave her the note and waited in silence while she read it.
Ines put a hand over her mouth.
[soft]"My grandmother always said my grandfather was the least punctual man in the world."[/soft]
She laughed through her tears.
"Would you like me to set it to the exact time?" the clockmaker asked.
Ines thought for a moment and shook her head.
"No. Leave it with its seven **minutes**. They belong to them."
Since then, Ines's house has a clock that runs seven minutes late, and nobody **minds**. [down]
