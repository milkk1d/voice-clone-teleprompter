# The shortcut
@energy 2
> Narrate with gentle humor.

Mateo trusted his phone more than any person.
So when his grandmother Elena suggested walking to the lake, he opened the map.
"It says forty minutes through the woods," he announced, very sure of himself.
His grandmother preferred the long way, the one she had known since she was a girl.
Mateo insisted: "Why walk two hours if there's a shortcut?" [up]
Elena agreed, though she slipped her father's old compass into her pocket.
At first, everything was perfect.
The **blue** dot moved across the screen, just as they moved among the trees.
Sounding like an expert, he explained that the phone listened to satellites flying very high.
"It works even without a **signal**," he said proudly.
His grandmother just raised an eyebrow.

# The woods
@energy 3
> Worry grows.

An hour later, there was still no sign of the lake.
The trail had turned into a maze of fallen logs, mud and dry branches.
Mateo turned the phone from side to side, confused.
The blue dot jumped from place to place, as if it were **dizzy**.
"It's the trees; they block the signals," he muttered.
Then the screen went black.
[pause 2s]
The battery had hit **zero**.
Mateo felt a chill in his stomach.
The wind shook the treetops with a strange sound.
It was starting to get dark, and he had no idea where the house was.
In a small voice, he asked: "So what do we do now?" [up]
His grandmother took the compass out of her pocket without saying a **word**.

# The compass
@energy 4
> The climax: relief and joy.

"It's a very old rule: water always runs down toward the lake," Elena explained.
"If we follow the **stream**, we'll get there."
They walked beside the water, listening to it rush over the stones.
His grandmother checked the compass every so often and corrected their course.
Her grandson didn't say a word for a long while.
Only their footsteps, the water and a hidden bird broke the silence.
[pause 2s]
Then, between the trees, a **silver** glint appeared.
"The lake!" Mateo shouted, running toward the shore.
On the other side were the dock and his grandmother's house, with the light on.
"Grandma, you're better than any app!"
Elena laughed and ruffled his hair.
"Apps are useful, but you also have to look **around** you."

# What he learned
@energy 2
> A quiet tone. End with affection.

That night, Mateo charged his phone and looked up why the blue dot had gone crazy.
He read that the system needs to see four satellites or more, whenever possible.
Among tall trees, buildings or mountains, the signals bounce and the calculation **fails**.
The next day, he downloaded a map of the woods and bought a spare battery.
But he did something more.
He asked his grandmother to teach him how to use the compass.
[soft]She gave him her father's, a small treasure worn smooth by the years.[/soft]
"Now it's your turn to look after it," she told him.
He still uses the map on his phone, especially in the city.
But every time he goes into the woods, his grandmother's **compass** goes with him. [down]
