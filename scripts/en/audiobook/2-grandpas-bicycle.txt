# The garage
@energy 2
> Narrate tenderly, without hurry.

Grandpa Ernesto kept a red bicycle in the garage, covered in dust.
Sofia found it one Saturday while looking for a box of Christmas decorations.
It had flat tires, a rusty chain and a broken seat.
Sofia asked out loud: "Whose bicycle is this?" [up]
Her grandfather leaned in the doorway and smiled **slowly**.
"It was mine. I used to ride it to the sea when I was your age."
He told her how he crossed the village pedaling, with the wind in his face.
Then came work, family and the years, and the bicycle was forgotten.
"It's beyond **repair**," he said with a shrug.
Sofia didn't answer.
But that night, in her notebook, she wrote a secret **plan**.
In one month, her grandfather would turn eighty.

# The missing piece
@energy 3
> The tension grows: something could go wrong.

Every afternoon, Sofia went to see Raul, the neighborhood mechanic, at his workshop.
Together they sanded off the rust, painted the frame and changed the wheels.
"You've got good hands," he told her, watching her adjust the brakes.
But there was a problem: the left pedal was **cracked** in two.
The model was so old that nobody made spare parts anymore.
They searched in shops, at flea markets and online, with no luck.
[pause 2s]
There were three days left until the birthday.
Sofia sat in the workshop doorway, ready to give up.
Just at that moment, Raul appeared with a **wooden** box full of old parts.
"I've kept this for forty years," he said, blowing off the dust.
Inside, among screws and bolts, was an **identical** pedal.

# The birthday
@energy 4
> The most moving moment: let your voice light up.

On the day of the party, the house was full of people, balloons and the smell of cake.
His granddaughter waited for everyone to finish singing, then asked for silence.
"Grandpa, close your eyes," she said, taking his hand.
She led him to the backyard, where the bicycle gleamed in the sun.
[pause 2s]
He opened his eyes and said **nothing** for a long time.
He touched the handlebars, the new seat and the bell, as if they were made of glass.
"It's the same one!" he exclaimed at last, his voice trembling.
"The same one I had when I was twelve!"
Everyone clapped as he climbed on, carefully, with Sofia's help.
He rode a **full** lap around the yard, laughing with pleasure like a child.
Nobody dared tell him to be careful.

# The photo
@energy 2
> Intimate and warm. End with a smile.

That evening, he asked Sofia to sit beside him.
He lowered his voice and asked: "Do you know why I stopped riding?" [up]
She shook her head.
[soft]"Because I thought it was too late for the things that made me happy."[/soft]
Sofia hugged him tight.
That week they took a photo together by the sea, with the red bicycle between them.
He hung it on the living room wall.
Underneath, in his **big**, shaky handwriting, he wrote one sentence.
"It's never too late to start riding again."
Today, the bicycle is still in the garage, but it no longer gathers dust.
Every Sunday, someone takes it for a ride down to the **beach**. [down]
