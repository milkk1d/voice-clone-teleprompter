# The attic
@energy 2
> A low, dusty voice.

When Elena inherited her great-aunt's house, she found the attic locked with a padlock.
Nobody in the family knew where the key was.
One rainy afternoon, she decided to break it with a hammer.
The door opened with a long groan.
Inside, it smelled of dust, old paper and oil paint.
There were trunks, broken chairs and an easel covered with a sheet.
Elena pulled the sheet away, without hurry.
[pause 2s]
Underneath was an oil portrait of a young **woman**.
She wore a blue dress, a pearl necklace and the hint of a smile.
Her eyes seemed to follow Elena anywhere in the attic.
**Who** was that woman, and why had someone hidden her? [up]

# The changes
@energy 2
> Slow. The tension builds.

[slow][soft]Elena brought the portrait downstairs and hung it in the living room.
It seemed a shame to leave it in the dark.[/soft][/slow]
That night, before bed, she looked at it for a long time.
The next morning, something was strange.
The woman's **smile** seemed a little wider.
Elena told herself it was the light.
But on the third day, the pearl necklace was gone from the painting.
And on the fifth, the blue dress had turned **gray**.
[pause 2s]
Elena examined the painting with a magnifying glass.
There were no new brushstrokes, no traces of fresh paint.
How could a painting change without anyone touching it? [up]

# The letters
@energy 3
> Very slow. Every detail is unsettling.

Looking for answers, Elena went back to the attic.
At the bottom of a trunk, she found a small treasure: a bundle of letters tied with a ribbon.
They were from her great-aunt, written when she was twenty.
Almost a century had passed since then.
In one of them, she wrote about the portrait.
[soft]"An artist I met at the harbor painted it. He said it would keep my soul."[/soft]
In another, written years later, the handwriting trembled.
"The painting grows old in my place. But I can no longer look at it."
Elena dropped the letter.
She remembered her great-aunt: a woman of ninety with the skin of a young girl.
[pause 2s]
And in the last letter, a single sentence, written in **red** ink.
"If anyone hangs it in the light again, the painting will look for another **face**."

# The mirror
@energy 4
> The reveal: firm, no shouting.

Elena ran downstairs to the living room.
The portrait was still hanging above the fireplace.
[pause 2s]
But the woman in the painting was no longer her great-aunt.
She had short hair, a wool jacket and tired **eyes**.
They were Elena's eyes.
It was her own face, painted in oil, down to every detail.
Even the small mole next to her mouth.
[slow][soft]Elena walked to the hallway mirror, trembling.[/soft][/slow]
Her reflection showed her a smooth face, without a single wrinkle, as if she were ten years younger.
Was it a gift, or a **curse**? [up]
Behind her, in the painting, the woman was smiling.

# The end
@energy 2
> Very short. Leave the doubt hanging.

Elena covered the portrait with the sheet again and carried it up to the attic.
She locked the door with a new padlock.
But sometimes, at night, someone can be heard **laughing** upstairs.
