# The empty house
@energy 2
> A low, slow voice, like telling a secret at night.

At the end of Poplar Street there's a house **nobody** has lived in for years.
It has dirty windows, a dry garden and a rusty gate set in a stone wall.
The neighbors say the last owner moved away without saying goodbye to anyone.
Nobody knows where she went.
Lucas walks past that house every night, on his way home from work.
He always walks a little faster on that stretch.
He always looks the other way.
But that winter night, something made him look.
[pause 2s]
In the second-floor window, a light was **on**.
A yellow, warm light, like a table lamp.
Who could be in there, if the house had been closed for years? [up]

# The light
@energy 2
> Slow. The tension builds.

[slow][soft]Lucas stopped by the gate.
Nobody was out at that hour.
The street was silent, except for the hum of a streetlight.
The light in the window didn't flicker.
There was something unusual about it.
It was still and steady, as if someone were reading beside it.
He looked for a simple explanation, but couldn't find one.
Then he saw a **shadow** pass behind the curtain.[/soft][/slow]
The hair on his arms stood up.
He thought about calling someone, but what would he say? [up]
"There's a light on in an empty house" didn't sound like an emergency.
So he did something he had never done before.
He pushed the gate, which creaked long and sharp, like a complaint.
The light on the second floor went **out** all at once.

# The door
@energy 3
> Very slow, every detail counts.

[slow]The front door was ajar.[/slow]
Lucas pushed it open with his fingertips.
Inside, it smelled of dust, old wood and something sweet, like cinnamon.
The air inside was icy.
He turned on his phone's flashlight.
The furniture was covered with white sheets.
On the dining table there was a cup of tea.
[soft]It was still **warm**.[/soft]
From upstairs came a soft sound: a slow tune, from a music box.
Lucas climbed the stairs, step by step.
Each board creaked under his feet.
The music was coming from the room at the end of the hall.
[pause 2s]
And the door to that room was **locked**.

# The reveal
@energy 4
> The reveal: tense, but no shouting.

Lucas knocked on the door three times.
The music stopped.
[pause 2s]
A woman's voice, calm and old, said from inside: "You're late, Lucas."
He took a step back.
He had never told anyone on that street his name.
The key turned in the lock on its own, and the door swung open.
The room was **empty**.
There was only a lamp switched on, an armchair and an open music box.
On the armchair lay a letter with his name written by hand.
[slow][soft]The handwriting was his **grandmother's**, and she had died ten years before.[/soft][/slow]
How could she have known this house? [up]

# The end
@energy 2
> Very short. Leave the doubt hanging.

Lucas never opened the letter.
He keeps it in a drawer, where it still is today.
But every night, when he walks past that house, the second-floor light comes **on**.
As if someone were **waiting** for him.
