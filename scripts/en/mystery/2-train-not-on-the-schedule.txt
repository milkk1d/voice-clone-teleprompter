# The station
@energy 2
> A low, nighttime voice.

Clara missed the last train at a quarter past eleven.
She watched it pull away from the stairs, her bag half closed and her **heart** racing.
The station emptied in a minute.
There was no one left on the platform.
Only a clock going tick tock and a coffee machine switched off.
She looked at the schedule taped to the wall.
The next train left at six in the morning.
Seven hours on a metal bench, with a cold that crept into her shoes.
She sat down, hugged her knees and closed her eyes.
[pause 2s]
At midnight, she heard a **whistle** in the distance.
A train was coming.
But what train, if there was none on the schedule? [up]

# The train
@energy 2
> Slow, with detail.

[slow][soft]The train stopped without a sound.
It was old, made of dark wood, with brass lamps in every car.
The lamps glowed with the light of another century.
The windows were fogged up, and inside, silhouettes of people moved.
A door opened in front of Clara, like an **invitation**.[/soft][/slow]
A conductor in a gray uniform leaned out.
He had a pale face and a kind smile.
"Coming, miss? This train will take you home."
Clara hesitated.
The station clock read twelve, but its hands weren't moving.
She picked up her bag and climbed aboard.
The door closed behind her without a **single** sound.

# The car
@energy 3
> Very slow. The tension rises.

Inside it was warm and smelled of lavender.
The passengers sat in silence, staring out the windows.
There was a woman in a feathered hat, a boy with a top and an old man with a newspaper.
Clara noticed something strange about that newspaper.
Its date was from fifty years ago.
[soft]Nobody spoke. Nobody looked at her.[/soft]
Outside the window, the usual landscape was gone.
Instead there were wheat fields under a huge moon, and houses with **chimneys** glowing.
Places Clara didn't know, but that felt strangely familiar.
[pause 2s]
Then the conductor came over and asked for her **ticket**.
Clara searched her bag and found one she had never bought.

# The reveal
@energy 4
> The reveal: firm, no shouting.

The ticket was made of yellow paper, with faded letters.
It had her name written in blue ink.
And a date from **fifty** years ago.
[pause 2s]
"This ticket belonged to your grandmother," the conductor said in a low voice.
"She bought it to go home, but that night she fell asleep at the station."
"Since then, this train comes every year, at the same hour, waiting for someone to use it."
Clara felt a lump in her throat.
Her grandmother had told her that story many times, and she had always thought it was a **fairy tale**.
[slow]The train began to slow down.[/slow]
Outside, she could see the station of her hometown, lit up as if for a party.
Could all of this be real? [up]

# The end
@energy 2
> Very short. Leave the doubt hanging.

Clara woke up on the metal bench before dawn.
The first train at six arrived right on time.
In her hand was a **yellow** ticket with her name on it.
And on the schedule, for the first time, there was a new line: twelve o'clock.
