# The call
@energy 2
> A low voice, like in a dark bedroom.

At three in the morning, in total darkness, Tomas's phone buzzed on the nightstand.
He looked at it with one eye half open.
The number on the screen had only four digits: one, nine, nine, six.
It wasn't a usual number.
It looked more like a **year**.
He thought about not answering, but the phone kept buzzing, insistent.
On the fifth ring, he swiped.
His own breathing sounded too loud in the silence.
"Hello?"
On the other end, he could hear rain and a distant radio.
[pause 2s]
And then, a voice he **knew** very well.
It was his own voice, when he was ten years old.

# The voice
@energy 2
> Slow. The tension builds.

[slow][soft]"Hello, is anyone there? I need help."[/soft][/slow]
Tomas sat up in bed, his heart pounding in his chest.
That child's voice was **his**.
It was impossible, but he had no doubt at all.
"What's happening?" he asked, almost in a whisper.
"The power went out and I'm in the kitchen with my brother. Dad isn't home and I'm scared."
All at once, Tomas remembered that night.
A storm, a blackout and two frightened brothers in the dark.
It had happened thirty years ago.
How could he be hearing it now? [up]
[pause 2s]
"Go to the fuse box, next to the **door**," Tomas said without thinking.

# The night of the blackout
@energy 3
> Very slow, every word matters.

On the other end came small footsteps and the creak of a door.
Then, a click.
[soft]And the distant radio went silent.[/soft]
"The lights are back!" said the boy, relieved.
"Thank you, sir. Who are you?"
Tomas didn't know what to say; he had never imagined that question.
He remembered that night **perfectly**.
He remembered dialing a random number in the dark, and an adult voice guiding him.
A voice he never knew who it belonged to.
[pause 2s]
Until **now**.
The call ended.
On the screen, the number vanished as if it had never existed.

# The reveal
@energy 4
> The reveal: firm, no shouting.

Tomas turned on the lamp and stared at the phone.
His hands were shaking.
He felt an icy chill down his back.
He searched his memory for every detail of that night.
The boy had dialed four random numbers, the first ones that came to mind.
[pause 2s]
One, nine, nine, six: the year of that very **night**.
Tomas opened his call log to make sure he hadn't dreamed it.
The call was still there, with the exact time: three in the morning.
[slow][soft]He had helped the child he once was, from the adult he is now.[/soft][/slow]
Or had it always been this way, from the very beginning? [up]
That **question** kept him awake until dawn.

# The end
@energy 2
> Very short. Leave the doubt hanging.

Since then, Tomas sleeps with his **phone** on.
Not because he expects another call.
Not because he's afraid of the dark.
But because one night, he may be the one who has to **make** it.
